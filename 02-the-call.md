# The call

The confession, both sides. No spoken audio was generated. Dialogue was laid in afterwards and the performance was prompted as reaction only.

---

## 2A. Thane's side (30s, six shots)

Uses the **THANE present day** and **LIGHTING present day UK** blocks, plus:

> No spoken audio, no lip-sync. Performance carried by expression and posture.

Each shot is timed against a line of dialogue that is never spoken on camera.

**Shot 1 (0–5s) Connected** *(beat: "Hi")*
Over-the-shoulder on the laptop. The call view opens. His self-view panel sits in the lower-right corner, the far-side panel soft and out of focus, never resolving. Cut to a medium close-up as the screen light shifts warmer. He lifts one hand in a small, slightly awkward wave. A quick, unsteady smile.

**Shot 2 (5–10s) Recognition** *(beat: "It's been a while")*
Tight close-up, gentle handheld drift. He listens. His head tilts a fraction, eyebrows rising in surprise, then a short laugh through the nose. He touches his own jaw briefly, glancing down and away, then back with a warmer smile. The stiffness in his shoulders drops.

**Shot 3 (10–15s) Easing in** *(beats: "You too" / "Has it been two years?")*
Medium close-up, slightly low angle. He answers with a small shrug and a grin, gesturing loosely toward the screen. Then his expression changes as he listens. The grin fades into something quieter. He looks down at the desk, nodding slowly.

**Shot 4 (15–20s) The years** *(beat: "Yes. Two years.")*
Tight close-up. He nods once, firmly, then again more slowly. His eyes drift off-frame past the camera, a long look at nothing. He blinks, refocuses, and gives a small pressed-lip smile that does not reach his eyes.

**Shot 5 (20–25s) The turn** *(beats: "What is it?" / "There's something I need to say")*
Medium close-up, camera drifting a fraction closer. His posture changes. He sits up, squares his shoulders, hands coming together on the desk. The smile goes. He looks directly into the screen, holds a beat, then begins to speak with visible care, stopping himself mid-sentence. His fingers press together.

**Shot 6 (25–30s) Waiting on her** *(beat: "Go on")*
Tight close-up, very still frame. He waits. As her answer lands, his eyes lift and steady. A slow breath. Everything in his face settles into resolve. He opens his mouth to begin. Screen glow brightens gently. Hold. Cut to black.

## 2B. Neethal's side (30s, six shots)

Uses the **NEETHAL** and **LIGHTING Neethal's room** blocks.

**Shot 1 (0–5s) Answering**
Medium close-up. She sits up against the headboard, hair loose and slightly disordered from sleep, screen light washing over her face. She rubs one eye, then smiles, easy and unguarded. She shifts the laptop on her knees.

**Shot 2 (5–10s) Warmth**
Close-up. She laughs at something, tucking hair behind one ear. Head tilts. Relaxed, familiar, the ease of someone talking to an old friend. Warm lamp rimming her hair from behind.

**Shot 3 (10–15s) The shift**
Close-up, camera drifting a fraction closer. The laugh fades. She goes still, listening. Her smile stays for a moment longer than the feeling behind it, then goes. Eyes fixed on the screen.

**Shot 4 (15–20s) Taking it in**
Tight close-up. She does not move. A slow blink. Her eyes drop briefly, then lift back. Lips part slightly, close again. The screen glow shifts subtly across her face.

**Shot 5 (20–25s) Everything at once**
Tight close-up, very still frame. Her eyes fill. She presses her lips together, breathes in. One hand comes up near her collarbone. She shakes her head very slightly, not refusal, disbelief.

**Shot 6 (25–30s) Sunrise**
Tight close-up. A tear breaks free and runs. Then, slowly, a smile spreads across her face, wide and involuntary. She laughs through it. Hold on the smile in the screen glow. Fade to black.

## 2C. Her answer (30s, six shots)

Picks up immediately after 2B.

**Shot 1 (0–5s) The break**
Tight close-up, static. The held breath releases. A tear runs, and she laughs through it, a short involuntary laugh, half sob. She wipes under one eye with the back of her hand and shakes her head slowly.

**Shot 2 (5–10s) She answers**
Close-up, camera drifting a fraction closer. She speaks, short and unhurried, a few words with a pause in the middle. Her eyebrows lift on the second phrase. The smile stays through all of it.

**Shot 3 (10–15s) Wider**
Medium shot. The room opens up around her: bed, headboard, lamp, soft bokeh behind. She is small in the frame, laptop light on her, one hand pressed flat against her chest. She looks down, then back up, still smiling.

**Shot 4 (15–20s) Stillness**
Close-up, very still frame. The laughing settles. She just looks at the screen, steady, eyes shining. No performance. A slow blink.

**Shot 5 (20–25s) Light change**
Close-up. The screen glow shifts subtly warmer and brighter across her face, as if the light on the other end has changed. Her expression softens further. She tilts her head slightly, resting it against the headboard, not looking away.

**Shot 6 (25–30s) Out**
Slow push in to a tight close-up, ending on her eyes. She holds the gaze. The frame settles. Warm lamp rimming her hair, screen light on her face. Hold two full seconds. Fade to black.

## Negative prompt

```
second face in focus, incoming video feed, other people in frame, resolved face
on the far side, subtitles, captions, any readable text, logos, brand names,
watermarks, celebrity likeness, changing face shape, hair length changing,
warped glasses, distorted mouth, teeth artefacts, extra fingers, fast camera moves,
exaggerated expressions
```

---

## Notes

The far-side participant panel is specified as **soft, dim and out of focus, no discernible face** in every shot of 2A. That keeps the other character off screen without needing a second consistent identity, and it reads as distance rather than as a limitation.

Writing the dialogue beats into the prompt as timing cues, with no lip-sync requested, produced far cleaner mouth movement than asking the model to form specific words. Generic speech rhythm cuts convincingly under any dialogue laid in afterwards.
