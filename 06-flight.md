# Flight

The journey out. He leaves to outrun the feeling and it follows him.

---

## 6A. The window (30s, six shots)

**Continuity, pasted identically into every shot:**

> A 24-year-old South Asian man with short black hair, thin dark-rimmed glasses, light stubble, wearing a plain cream button-up shirt. He sits in a window seat on a commercial aircraft. Original character, generic appearance, not based on any real or known person. Identical face, glasses and clothing in every shot.
>
> 16:9, 4K. Daylight above cloud level. Cool white light flooding in from the window as the key, deep shadow in the cabin interior. Low-saturation grade, teal shadows, soft blacks, fine grain, shallow depth of field, cabin interior falling into bokeh. Plain unbranded grey cabin fittings, plain grey seat fabric. Natural human motion. Expression held steady, calm, still, lips closed.

**Shot 1 (0–5s) Settled**
Medium shot from the aisle side, slightly behind. He sits low in the seat, head turned toward the window. Cabin dim around him, the window a bright rectangle of white. Light spills across one side of his face and shoulder. Very slight motion in the frame, the aircraft's subtle movement.

**Shot 2 (5–10s) The window**
Over-the-shoulder, shallow focus. His shoulder and the curve of the window frame soft in the foreground. Beyond the glass: a field of cloud, flat white, a pale horizon line. Focus sits on the view, not on him.

**Shot 3 (10–15s) Close**
Tight close-up on his face at the window, side-lit. Cloud light reflected in his lenses, moving slowly. He blinks once. His eyes track something out there and lose it. Expression unchanged.

**Shot 4 (15–20s) Hand on glass**
Close on his hand resting against the window frame, fingertips touching the inner pane. Cold white light on his knuckles. The hand stays. Cabin darkness behind.

**Shot 5 (20–25s) Reflection**
Close-up shot through the window glass from outside the frame line. His face faintly reflected in the pane, overlaid on cloud. Two images of him, one solid, one ghosted in the glass. He does not move.

**Shot 6 (25–30s) Away**
Wide, from the aisle. He turns his head forward, away from the window, and rests it back against the seat. Eyes close. The bright rectangle of the window sits beside him, unwatched. Hold. Fade out.

**Return flight variant:** change the window light to **warm golden** rather than cool white, put a coastline or city grid below instead of flat cloud, and in shot 6 have him turn toward the window and stay there rather than closing his eyes. Same shots, opposite meaning.

## 6B. The mistaken face (20s, four shots)

Adds a flight attendant. She is kept as backlit silhouette and out-of-focus edge until the reveal, so the near-miss reads and no second consistent identity is needed.

**Added to the continuity block:**

> A flight attendant: a South Asian woman in a plain unbranded dark navy uniform, hair pulled back neatly, holding a plain plastic cup. Generic appearance, not based on any real or known person.

**Shot 1 (0–5s) The approach**
Medium shot from the window side. He sits with his head against the seat, eyes half closed. In the aisle behind him, a figure moves into frame, heavily out of focus, backlit by the cabin lights, only a silhouette of a woman with long dark hair loose over one shoulder. She leans in. He begins to turn.

**Shot 2 (5–10s) The mistake**
Tight close-up on his face as he turns. His eyes open properly, and for a beat everything in his expression changes, a lift, a flicker of recognition, breath caught. Genuine, involuntary. Behind him the figure remains soft and unresolved, hair catching the backlight. He is looking at someone he thinks he knows.

**Shot 3 (10–15s) The reveal**
Focus racks off him and onto her. She resolves into a flight attendant in a plain navy uniform, hair pulled back tight and neat, holding out a plain cup of water. Polite professional smile. Nothing like the silhouette suggested. She says something short and unremarkable, mouth moving without sound.

**Shot 4 (15–20s) Back down**
Cut to his face. The lift drains out of it in a single beat. He takes the cup, gives a small polite nod, and a flat brief smile that does not reach his eyes. She moves out of frame. He turns back to the window, holding the cup without drinking. Bright white window light across his face. Hold. Fade out.

**Note:** the hair does the deception. **Loose over one shoulder** in the silhouette, **pulled back tight** in the reveal. It is the same detail from Neethal's character lock, which makes the mistake feel earned rather than arbitrary.

Shot 2 is the only shot in the whole flight sequence where his expression is allowed to change. Everything before it has been held steady, so the flicker reads as enormous.
