# Camera studies

Orbits and the dolly zoom. These are the shots where the method matters more than the content.

The rule that came out of them: **split long moves into short passes.** A full 360 in one 30 second generation drifts badly past about 120 degrees. The far side of the rotation is where identity slips, wrong ear shape, glasses arms that do not match, collars that change. Three 10 second passes of 120 degrees each hold the likeness far better and can be dissolved or cut together.

---

## 5A. Thane, 360 orbit, left (30s, three passes)

**Continuity, pasted identically into every pass:**

> A 24-year-old South Asian man with short black hair, thin dark-rimmed glasses, light stubble, wearing a plain cream button-up shirt. He sits in a chair in a small dim bedroom at night. Original character, generic appearance, not based on any real or known person.
>
> 16:9, 4K. Dim cool-blue ambient light, one warm lamp glow from camera left, soft cool white light from a source in front of him. Low-saturation grade, teal shadows, soft blacks, fine grain, shallow depth of field. Natural human motion. No audio, mouth moves in a relaxed natural speech rhythm, lips soft, catching the light.

**Pass 1 (0–10s), 0° to 120°**
Medium close-up, head and chest. Camera starts straight-on and orbits smoothly to his left, ending at profile. He is mid-sentence, speaking unhurriedly with small pauses. Eyes forward and slightly down. He glances away once, then back. One hand lifts in a small gesture and lowers. The warm lamp accent slides across his cheek as the angle changes.

**Pass 2 (10–20s), 120° to 240°**
Camera continues past his shoulder and around behind him. His silhouette rims against the soft light in front of him. He shifts in the chair, shoulders tightening, one hand moving to the back of his neck. Camera continues around to his right side. Lighting inverts naturally, the front light now rimming him from behind, ambient room tone filling the near side.

**Pass 3 (20–30s), 240° to 360°**
Camera completes the orbit back to straight-on and eases to a stop. His speech rhythm slows, with a longer pause. His eyes lift and settle forward as the camera arrives front-on. A small breath. His expression steadies. Hold on the final frame.

**Motion:** one continuous smooth orbit per pass, constant speed, level height, subject centred. No shake, no speed ramps, no cuts within a pass.

**Note:** pass 2 is the one to generate first. If the back-of-head section holds up, the other two will. It is also where the most can be hidden, since the face is barely visible.

---

## 5B. Thane, 360 orbit, right

Identical to 5A with the direction reversed: the camera travels to his right, passes behind, and returns from his left.

The look changes in one meaningful way. The warm lamp sits at camera left, so orbiting right means **losing the warm side early and regaining it at the end**, rather than carrying it across the front half. That gives a darker middle and a warmer arrival, which suits a shot ending on resolve.

Add to the negative prompt: `camera reversing direction mid-shot`.

## 5C. Neethal, 360 orbit (30s, three passes)

**Continuity, pasted identically into every pass:**

> A 24-year-old South Asian woman with long dark wavy hair worn loose over one shoulder, a small dark bindi, minimal makeup, wearing a soft cream cotton kurta. Warm medium-brown skin, calm oval face, dark eyes. She sits up in bed against a headboard in a small dim bedroom at night. Original character, generic appearance, not based on any real or known person.
>
> 16:9, 4K. Dim warm ambient light, one warm bedside lamp glow from camera right, soft cool white light from a source in front of her. Low-saturation grade, soft blacks, fine grain, shallow depth of field. Natural human motion. No audio, she is listening, lips closed and soft, catching the light.

**Pass 1 (0–10s), 0° to 120°**
Medium close-up, head and shoulders. Camera starts straight-on and orbits smoothly to her left, ending at profile. She is listening intently, very still. Her eyes stay forward. She blinks slowly once, and one hand rises to tuck hair behind her ear, then lowers. The warm lamp accent slides across her cheek as the angle changes, loose hair catching the light in strands.

**Pass 2 (10–20s), 120° to 240°**
Camera continues past her shoulder and around behind her. Her silhouette and the fall of her hair rim against the soft light in front of her. She shifts slightly against the headboard, one shoulder dropping. Camera continues around to her right side. Lighting inverts naturally.

**Pass 3 (20–30s), 240° to 360°**
Camera completes the orbit back to straight-on and eases to a stop. She remains still, but her eyes fill as the camera arrives front-on. She presses her lips together, breathes in. The beginning of a smile. Hold on the final frame.

**Note:** hair is the drift risk here rather than glasses. Long loose hair changes length and parting between angles more readily than anything else, so it is called out in both the lock and the negatives.

## 5D. Dolly zoom, white void (15s, three shots)

The effect is described mechanically. Naming it rarely works.

**Continuity:**

> A 24-year-old South Asian man with short black hair, thin dark-rimmed glasses, light stubble, wearing a plain cream button-up shirt. Original character, generic appearance, not based on any real or known person.
>
> He stands alone, centred, in a completely empty white void, a seamless infinite white background with no walls, no floor line, no horizon, no objects, no shadows on the backdrop. Soft even diffused white light from all sides, with one slightly stronger soft key from camera left shaping his face. Clean high-key look, gentle falloff, faint film grain, subtle halation. 16:9, 4K.

**Shot 1 (0–5s) Establish**
Medium shot, straight-on, static camera. He stands still, arms at his sides, looking directly into the lens. Neutral, composed. Very slight natural movement, breath, one slow blink. The white void surrounds him completely.

**Shot 2 (5–10s) The move**
The camera physically moves toward him while the lens simultaneously zooms out. He remains exactly the same size in frame throughout, head and shoulders locked in the same position, while the space around him visibly stretches and expands, the perspective distorting outward, the field of view widening around a fixed subject. Continuous, smooth, single unbroken move. He does not move.

**Shot 3 (10–15s) Hold**
Camera settles. He remains centred in a now much wider, emptier white frame, smaller in negative space, though his scale on screen never changed. He holds the look into the lens. A slow blink. Very still.

**Negative:** subject changing size in frame, subject moving, camera shake, cuts mid-move, walls, corners, floor lines, horizon, shadows on the backdrop, grey or coloured background, other people, objects, text, logos, watermarks, warped glasses, changing face shape, extra fingers, lens flares.

**Note:** this one is unreliable. Most generations produce a plain push-in and ignore the counter-zoom. The dependable route is a static wide plate with the scale animated in post and the background pushed on a separate layer.

A second problem worth flagging: a pure white void gives the perspective stretch nothing to distort against, so even a successful generation is hard to read. A faint floor shadow or a subtle gradient makes the effect visible.
